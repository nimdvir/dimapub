The Market Opportunity: Solving the "Two-Book Problem"

Currently, to teach a business-facing database course, instructors are forced to assign a two-book bundle, creating an inherent "stitching tax" on course design:

Book 1: Theory

Database Systems 14e (Coronel) is used to teach relational theory, normalization, and database concepts, but proves too abstract and engineering-focused for non-CS students.

Book 2: Execution

Illustrated Microsoft 365 1e (Beskeen) is used to teach students how to build forms and queries in Access, but functions as a software manual with no theoretical depth.

The Solution: Using Data to Drive Business Performance replaces this fragmented experience. It is a unified, single-textbook solution that integrates database theory directly with hands-on business execution, tailored specifically for the modern business undergraduate.

Key Differentiators & Pedagogical Innovation

The "Design Pivot"

Traditional texts front-load complex normalization (1NF-3NF) early on, demoralizing business students. This structure allows students to use data first—building confidence and context—before pivoting to designing schemas in Chapter 9.

Real SQL vs. QBE

While Access is a great starting point, modern employers demand real, executable SQL (the #1 requested data analyst skill). The text explicitly transitions students from local environments to modern dialects (PostgreSQL/SQLite).

"Earned AI" Architecture

Rather than slapping AI sidebars into old chapters, AI is taught as a structural consumer of databases—showing students that without clean schemas, AI hallucinations happen. It introduces modern vector/RAG concepts natively.

Major-Specific Career Translation

Chapter 16 explicitly maps database skills to specific business majors (Accounting, Finance, Marketing) and helps students translate lab work into resume-ready STAR interview answers—crucial for retention-focused business schools.

AI-Resistant Assessment

Built structurally post-ChatGPT. End-of-chapter problems aren't simple lookup questions; they are artifact-based (ERDs, normalized schemas, decision memos) applied to an unseen scenario (PetVax), solving the assignment integrity problem at the architectural level.

Revision Economics & Modularity

Tool-dependent content (Access, Supabase, BI) is quarantined to specific modules (Ch. 11, 12, 14, and appendices). The relational spine (Ch. 2-10) is tool-neutral, allowing for surgical, cost-effective updates rather than full revision cycles.

Departments under budget pressure are increasingly abandoning expensive, disjointed bundles for free (but limited) OER texts. A cohesive, digital-first product provides the unified quality required to win those seats back into the publisher ecosystem.
